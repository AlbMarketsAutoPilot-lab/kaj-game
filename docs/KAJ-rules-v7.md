# KAJ — Kris Ann's Journey: Game Rules (version 7, frozen)

Status: **concept frozen**. Everything below was approved by the owner.
This version replaces version 6 (`KAJ-rules-v6.md`, kept for history).
Items that need calculations or content work are listed in section 15 and will be done in the technical phase.

**Design principles**
- One turn = one action = one decision.
- Luck adds spice, but good play should win.
- The whole game is kid-friendly. Short games are the first step of the learning curve; longer games naturally attract experienced players.

---

## 1. Setup

- 2 players, each with their own colour. (4 players and 8 profiles may come later.)
- Game length: **30**, **50** or **100 turns**. Each length has its own map (section 2).
- Each player chooses a profile: Backpacker, Business Traveler, Luxury Traveler or Digital Nomad (section 12).
- Each player chooses a starting continent. Two players can never start on the same continent. Antarctica is not a starting continent.
- The map then zooms in to each player's continent, and each player chooses a starting area.
- **Welcome bonus:**

  | Starting continent | Bonus |
  |---|---|
  | Europe, Asia, Africa | +3 |
  | Americas | +4 |
  | Oceania | +5 |

## 2. Maps

### Three maps

| Game length | Playable areas |
|---|---|
| 30 turns | 45–50 (general areas, e.g. "Scandinavia", "Middle East", "USA East", "USA West") |
| 50 turns | 80–90 (more detail, but sensitive regions stay grouped) |
| 100 turns | 120–140 (mostly single countries, sensitive regions still grouped) |

- All maps are built from **one master list of countries** (shapes, borders, facts). Each map is a grouping of that list; for example, "Scandinavia" = Norway + Sweden + Denmark + Finland.
- Big countries are split into parts using their real state or province borders.

### What is shown

- The map is true to real geography, and there are **no grey areas**. Every place is one of these:
  - a playable area;
  - part of a bigger playable area (e.g. Israel and Palestine are always part of "Middle East"; Kosovo is part of "Balkans");
  - too small to see at world or continent zoom (e.g. Pacific islands);
  - never shown because it has no land border (e.g. **Taiwan**, **Cyprus**). When zoomed in, China's coast shows only the South China Sea.
- Only if a grey area truly cannot be avoided, clicking it shows: *"Kris Ann hasn't packed for this place yet. Coming in a future journey!"*

### Islands

| Place | How it is handled |
|---|---|
| Iceland, Japan, Australia, New Zealand | Playable island areas |
| UK & Ireland | One area called **"UK & Ireland"** on the 30- and 50-turn maps (never "British Isles"); UK and Ireland are separate areas on the 100-turn map |
| Greenland | Part of the Scandinavia/Denmark area |
| Hawaii, Alaska | Part of USA West |
| Sri Lanka | Part of the India area |
| Madagascar | Part of East Africa on the small maps; may be its own area on the 100-turn map |
| Cuba and the Caribbean | Part of Central America on the small maps; a "Caribbean" area (port) on the 100-turn map |
| Indonesia, Philippines, Papua New Guinea | A "Maritime Southeast Asia" area, reachable over land from Malaysia (Borneo) and by port |

### Rules that name a country

On grouped maps, any rule that names a country applies to **the area that contains it**.

### The view

- The game opens with a world view showing only the continents, then zooms in to each player's continent to choose a starting area.
- After that, the view shows **one area at a time**, with its neighbouring areas and oceans, and whether each has an airport or a port.
- A world map shows **both players' routes** (your own and your opponent's).

## 3. Scoring

| What | Points |
|---|---|
| New area fully visited | +1 |
| Repeat visit to an area | 0 (you only use the turn) |
| First arrival on a new continent (the starting continent does not count) | +2 |
| Wonder area: first visit (every player) | +1 extra |
| Big country split into **N** parts, all parts visited | +1 +**N** |

- **Big countries:** USA, Canada, Russia, China, Brazil, Australia. The number of parts depends on the map. For example, the 100-turn map has USA 3 parts (+3), Canada 4 (+4) and Russia 5 (+5); the 30-turn map has USA East and USA West (+2).
- Big countries are **all or nothing**: a partial visit gives 0 points, including no area point. The bonus can be earned only once.
- You can leave a big country halfway and come back later to **continue where you stopped** (you don't restart).
- **Points can never go below 0.**

### Wonders

| Map | Number of wonders |
|---|---|
| 30 turns | 6–7 |
| 50 turns | 10–11 |
| 100 turns | 15 |

- Big countries are never wonders.
- If an area contains two wonder countries (e.g. Italy and Greece on the small map), it counts as **one** wonder. The wonder list is adjusted for each map.
- The **first player to reach a wonder** gets the ⭐ **right to buy** its guided-tour business (section 6).

**The 15 wonders on the 100-turn map:**

| Continent | Wonder countries |
|---|---|
| Europe | Italy, Greece, France |
| Asia | India, Jordan, Cambodia, Japan, Turkey |
| Africa | Egypt, Tanzania, Morocco |
| North America | Mexico |
| South America | Peru, Chile |
| Oceania | New Zealand |

## 4. Movement

- **Walking:** 1 turn to move to a neighbouring area. Walking is always allowed and free (except entering a visa area, section 7).
- **Two players can never be in the same area at the same time.** A player may enter an area the opponent has left.

### Airports and ports

| Map | Airports | Ports |
|---|---|---|
| 30 turns | 6 | 4 |
| 50 turns | 8 | 4–5 |
| 100 turns | 10 | 5–6 |

- Every airport and every port has **one fixed destination**. Routes are **two-way pairs** (A ↔ B).
- The **Luxury Traveler** can choose **any** airport or port as a destination.
- **Plane:** 1 skipped turn (in the air), then you land.
- **Ship:** 3 turns at sea, then you arrive.
- Profiles change these times and the ticket price (section 12).

### Ticket: pay or airline quiz

1. You arrive in an area with an airport or port. This is one turn.
2. On a later turn you decide: walk on, or take the plane or ship.
3. If you take the plane or ship, you choose:
   - **pay the ticket** and board; or
   - try the **airline quiz**: *"Airline promotion: answer correctly and fly free!"* It is one a/b question about the **destination** (often a drawing: "Where is this landmark?"). **Each try uses that turn.** A correct answer means a free ticket.
4. After the **3rd wrong answer** you must pay and board.
5. **If you can't afford your ticket, you may keep trying the quiz each turn until you answer correctly.** This applies to every profile, including Luxury.

## 5. Ticket money

- The ticket price depends on the profile: **1 point** (Digital Nomad), **2 points** (Business), **3 points** (Luxury). The Backpacker never pays; it travels only with the quiz.
- If the **departure** airport or port has an owner, the whole ticket goes **to that owner**. It is paid once per trip, never twice.
- The owner also pays their own ticket to themselves when using their own airport or port. (There is no bank in the game.)
- A free ticket (quiz) pays nobody.

## 6. Businesses

| Business | Where | Price | What the owner earns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🏛️ Guided tours | Wonder area | **2** | **1 point** from every other player, each time they enter that area (the same 1 point for every profile) |
| ✈️ Airline | Airport | **3** | The ticket of every trip that departs from that airport (section 5) |
| ⛴️ Ferry agency | Port | **2** | The ticket of every trip that departs from that port (section 5) |

- **Buying:** the first player to arrive may buy. For a wonder, the first to arrive has the ⭐ right to buy. If they don't buy (or can't pay), the next player who arrives may buy.
- **Selling:** only to the other player, at **the same price it was bought for**. The other player answers Yes or No. Ownership is shown with an ownership card that moves to the new owner.
- Businesses are part of every game length.

## 7. Visas

- Entering an area where the **opponent has citizenship** costs a **2-point visa**.
- The visa is paid **every time you enter** that area. Moving inside it is free (this matters for big countries).
- **No money, no visa. There is no exception.** A player who can't pay can't enter. They can go back and find an airport or port, and try the quiz to travel free.

## 8. Citizenship

- Each time a player arrives in a new area, they are asked: "Ask for citizenship here? Yes / No". The Digital Nomad is never asked.
- A player can ask for citizenship **only once per game**.
- **Citizenship exam** (no event cards during the exam; the exam is the event):
  1. **Turn 1:** study **6 facts** about the country.
  2. **Turn 2:** answer **3 a/b questions** about those facts.
  3. **All 3 correct:** citizenship is granted on turn 3.
  4. **Any wrong answer:** one extra turn shows the wrong answers and the correct ones, then citizenship is granted (turn 4). Nobody loses the citizenship; failing costs only that extra turn.
- In online games there is a timer for reading and a timer for answering.
- The **Luxury Traveler** gets citizenship instantly, with no exam.
- Citizenship covers the **whole country or area**, including big countries.
- Once citizenship is granted, the opponent must pay a visa to enter that area (section 7).

## 9. Travel turns: mini-game or event card

On every travel turn (in the air or at sea), the player is asked: *"Another passenger would like to play a game with you. Play?"*

- **Yes:** play one skill mini-game. Win +1, lose −1 (in every game length).
- **No:** draw an event card.

**Skill mini-games** (no betting or chance games, to protect the app store age rating):
- "Which flag is this?"
- "Which country is bigger?"
- a memory game

Large board games (backgammon etc.) are **not** included.

## 10. Event cards

**When a card is drawn:**
- every 3 turns (30-turn game), 5 turns (50-turn game) or 10 turns (100-turn game)
- on a travel turn, if the player declines the mini-game

**Card strength:** mostly ±1, some ±2 or "lose a turn", rare ±5.

**Decks:**
- country cards (e.g. "You find a treasure in the pyramids of Egypt")
- plane cards
- ship cards (e.g. pirates, strong winds)
- Backpacker cards (e.g. "A truck takes you 2 areas further", "A friend pays your ticket")

**Keep cards:** some cards are kept and played when the player chooses (e.g. "Airport strike: opponent's next flight is delayed 1 turn", "Free visa"). A player can hold **at most 2** keep cards.

**Mini missions:** some cards give a short goal (e.g. "Reach a wonder within 5 turns: +3"). They appear only **from the second third of the game**:

| Game length | First mission turn |
|---|---|
| 30 turns | 11 |
| 50 turns | 18 |
| 100 turns | 35 |

**No extra cards at the end of the game.**

## 11. Blocking and escape

- A player can always walk back through areas already visited (0 points) to find new land, an airport or a port.
- Map rule for the technical phase: **every region that can be closed off by a single visa area must have its own airport or port**, so no player can be stuck forever.

## 12. Profiles

| Profile | Ticket | Plane | Ship | Advantage | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🎒 **Backpacker** | **Never pays** | 1 turn | 3 turns | **+3** for visiting 3 continents. Own helper cards | Plane and ship **only with the quiz** (unlimited tries, one per turn) |
| 💼 **Business Traveler** | **2 points** | **0 turns** | **1 turn** | **+3** for citizenship **in any area** | Higher ticket price |
| 💎 **Luxury Traveler** | **3 points** | **0 turns** | **1 turn** | Can fly or sail to **any** airport or port. **Instant citizenship**, no exam. **+5** for visiting 5 continents | Highest ticket price |
| 💻 **Digital Nomad** | 1 point | 1 turn | 3 turns | **+1 for every turn spent on a plane or ship** (works online) | Can't ask for citizenship. **−5** at the end if they visited fewer than 3 continents |

Every profile may use the airline quiz.

## 13. End of game

- The player with the most points wins.
- Tie-break: the player who visited more continents wins; if still tied, more areas.

## 14. Screens (no rule changes)

- **Passport:** a stamp for each area visited and a short fact about it. It uses one stamp template filled with the name and flag colours, and facts shown in rotation.
- **World map:** both players' routes.
- **Digital Nomad:** a "Continents 2/3" progress bar.
- **Backpacker tip (30-turn map):** when a Backpacker picks the Americas or Oceania, show "Tip: from Europe, Asia or Africa you can walk to 3 continents."

## 15. Content and technical phase

### Content

- **Facts:** **27–30 per country** in the master list (about 150 countries, roughly 4,000–4,500 facts). They are used by the exam, the airline quiz, the passport and the mini-games. The game tracks which facts each player has already seen, so new ones come first.
- **Drawings:** about **200–250** in total (minimum to launch about 120), stored inside the app. The owner draws the final art; placeholder drawings will be made for building and testing.

  | Use | Drawings |
  |---|---|
  | Landmark quiz (about 16–20 destinations × 8) | 130–160 |
  | Wonders | 15 |
  | Event card types | 20–25 |
  | Profiles + Kris Ann | 5 |
  | Business ownership cards | 3 |
  | Citizenship exam screens | 3 |
  | Mini-game backgrounds | ~5 |
  | Passport stamp template | 1 |
  | Icons | 20–30 |

- **Testing drawings:** with two a/b choices, a fair drawing should be recognised by about 70–90% of testers.

### Technical phase (needs calculations)

1. Exact area lists for the 3 maps, including big-country part counts and the wonder list per map.
2. Airport and port pairs per map, checked by calculation so that every island is reachable and **no player can ever get permanently stuck**, including behind a visa area.
3. Card texts for all decks, the fact database and the quiz and mini-game question banks.
4. The drawing list (what each drawing shows and where it is used).
5. A simulator to confirm the balance, especially **Luxury** (probably the strongest profile), the Backpacker, the Digital Nomad and business prices.

## Appendix: design analysis (estimates, to be confirmed by the simulator)

### Real travel cost compared with walking

Walking 1 turn earns about +1, so each lost turn counts as about 1 lost point.

| Profile | Plane | Ship |
|---|---|---|
| Normal (no profile) | ≈ −2 | ≈ −4 |
| Backpacker (quiz wait ~1.1–2 turns) | ≈ −2 to −3 | ≈ −4 to −5 |
| Business | ≈ −2 | ≈ −3 |
| Luxury | ≈ −3 (but any destination) | ≈ −4 |
| Digital Nomad | ≈ −1 | ≈ −1 |

- The **+2 new-continent bonus** makes trips to new continents worth it.
- The **2-point visa** costs about the same as going around by plane, so paying it or avoiding it are both real choices.

### Airline quiz

- An a/b question: **50%** when guessing, about **70–90%** for a player who knows geography.
- Each try uses a turn, so for a normal player the quiz costs about 1.1–2 turns on average. Paying is usually better; the quiz is worth it when stuck, short of points, or to avoid paying the opponent's airline.

### Citizenship exam (6 facts, 3 questions, all correct)

| Player | Chance to pass |
|---|---|
| Guessing | 12.5% |
| Well prepared | ~73–80% |

With 27–30 facts per country, a player can take about 5 different exams in the same country before facts repeat.

### Business returns (2 players, good location, bought early)

| Business (price) | Expected return, 50 turns | Expected return, 100 turns |
|---|---|---|
| Airline (3) | ~2–4 | ~4–8 |
| Ferry agency (2) | ~1–3 | ~2–6 |
| Guided tours (2) | ~1–3 (choke wonders) | ~2–5 |

Buying early in a long game pays back; buying late in a short game usually doesn't. Airlines are especially attractive for Luxury, which gets its own 3-point tickets back.

### Profile balance (50-turn game, compared with a player with no profile)

| Profile | Estimated edge |
|---|---|
| Backpacker | +3 to +4 |
| Business | +4 to +5 |
| Luxury | +6 to +8 (watch in the simulator) |
| Digital Nomad | +4 to +7 |

### Welcome bonus fairness

| Start | Extra cost compared with Europe/Asia/Africa | Bonus difference |
|---|---|---|
| Americas | ≈ −1 to −2 (at least one trip to reach the rest of the world) | +1 |
| Oceania | ≈ −2 to −3 (islands only; must fly or sail) | +2 |

### Luck vs skill

- Every game length has about 10 scheduled event cards (30÷3, 50÷5, 100÷10). The base score grows with game length, so **longer games reward skill more**.
- Quizzes, the exam and the skill mini-games add skill rather than luck.
- Earlier estimate of the chance that the better player wins (before quizzes; likely higher now):

| Game length | Better player wins |
|---|---|
| 30 turns | ~63% |
| 50 turns | ~72% |
| 100 turns | ~85% |
